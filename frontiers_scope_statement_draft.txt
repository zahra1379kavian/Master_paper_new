Draft scope statement for Frontiers submission (edit before submission):

This manuscript fits the Research Topic "Brain imaging and stimulation for motor control and movement disorders" because it uses task-based fMRI to study the neural organization of motor vigour in Parkinson's disease. The study addresses a central problem in movement disorders: whether trial-wise behavioural variability reflects instability in central vigour-related neural coding or whether part of the variability arises downstream of a relatively stable neural signal. Using a constrained optimization framework, the manuscript identifies a distributed brain projection that tracks reaction time while showing lower consecutive-trial variability than behaviour itself. The recovered network includes motor, striatal, cerebellar, temporal-limbic, and contextual regions, separating trial-wise vigour coding from broad task engagement. The medication analysis further shows that dopaminergic state changes the cross-subject organization of the recovered network, while the GVS analysis evaluates stimulation-related modulation within the same network. These features align directly with the topic's focus on brain imaging, motor control, movement disorders, neuromodulation, and inter-individual differences in therapeutic response.
